Golden Corral Colorado Springs: The Complete Buffet Guide (2026)

Planning a meal at Golden Corral in Colorado Springs should not take three browser tabs and a phone call. The restaurant doesn't post one fixed price list, and plenty of the numbers floating around online are years out of date. This guide lays out the address, hours, real prices, and menu for Golden Corral Colorado Springs in plain English, so you can plan the trip without guessing.

Where It Is and When It's Open

There is one Golden Corral serving Colorado Springs, and it sits on the east side of town in the Cimarron Hills area, a minute off Powers Boulevard. If you are heading to the Golden Corral buffet and grill Colorado Springs location, this is the address to put in your maps app. The old Woodmen Road site is closed for good, so ignore any listing that still points there.

One thing to know before you plan around breakfast: it's a Friday to Sunday deal here. The doors open at 7:30 AM on those three days and the morning spread usually runs until about 11. Monday through Thursday the restaurant opens at 10:30 AM straight into lunch, so a weekday breakfast run will leave you standing in the parking lot. Lunch runs until 4 PM, when dinner takes over and the sirloin comes out.

Holiday hours shift, so a quick look at the official Golden Corral location page before a holiday visit saves a wasted drive.

The building is a short hop from most of the city. Peterson Space Force Base is about 10 minutes away, the airport is roughly 12, and downtown takes around 15. Garden of the Gods runs about 20 minutes and the Air Force Academy closer to 25, which makes this a practical stop on the way in or out of town rather than a detour.

Senior Prices and the Early Bird Window

Seniors 60 and older typically pay a dollar or two less at every meal. The catch is that you have to ask. The senior rate is rarely posted on the board, and once you have paid, the cashier cannot go back and fix it.

The real saving is the weekday early bird. Many locations drop the senior buffet to roughly $9.99 to $10.99 on weekday afternoons, most often between 2 and 4 PM, often with a drink included. Pay before 4 PM and you can still be eating when the steak hits the line a few minutes later. Ask this location what window it runs, since it varies from store to store.

What's on the Buffet

The buffet carries close to 150 items and the lineup changes with the clock. Walk in at 8 AM and you meet an omelet station. Walk in at 6 PM and someone is carving steak in front of you. Knowing which window you are paying for matters more than most people expect.

Breakfast, Friday to Sunday

The omelet station is the draw. Fresh eggs folded around bacon, diced ham, sausage, peppers, mushrooms, onions, and cheddar, cooked while you wait. Alongside it sits scrambled eggs, bacon, sausage links and patties, biscuits and gravy, corned beef hash, skillet hash browns, pancakes, French toast, waffles, and cinnamon rolls. Cold cases hold fruit, yogurt, cereal, and donuts.

Lunch, From About 11 AM

The steakburger bar runs on weekdays until 4 PM, with patties grilled to order and served on the house yeast rolls. The hot line adds fried chicken, pot roast, meatloaf, Bourbon Street Chicken, fried and baked fish, pizza, and a rotating manager's special that might be pork loin, pot pie, or chicken and dumplings.

Dinner, From 4 PM

This is the meal worth planning around. USDA sirloin is carved and grilled to order every night, and you can ask for a fresh cut instead of taking whatever has been sitting under the lamp. The carving station turns out bone in turkey, Golden Delicious shrimp arrive breaded and crisp, and the rest of the line stays fully stocked.

Salad Bar and Dessert

The salad bar covers the basics well with greens, fresh vegetables, fruits, cheeses, meats, crackers, and a daily prepared salad that often includes seafood salad. Dessert is where this place earns its reputation. Carrot cake, cheesecake, chocolate cake, apple and blueberry pie, bread pudding, banana pudding, brownies, cookies, cotton candy, and soft serve with hot fudge. A chocolate fountain runs on the east side of this location, and it is exactly as popular with kids as you would guess.

Specific dishes rotate by day and season, so the full golden corral menu is worth a look before you promise someone a particular dish.

Weigh and Pay: Buffet Food by the Pound

Weigh and Pay suits anyone who wants the food without paying the sit down price. You build a box from the line, they weigh it, and you pay by weight.

  1. Pick up a to go box at the register.
  2. Fill it from the hot bar, salad bar, sides, or dessert. Mixing is allowed.
  3. Close the lid and carry it to the cashier.
  4. They weigh it and multiply by the current per pound rate.

Most locations charge between $6.99 and $8.99 per pound. A full box usually weighs 1.5 to 2.5 pounds, so a solid meal lands somewhere between $12 and $22.

Three rules keep the line moving. You have to use their container, since the scale is set for its weight. The box is strictly for takeout, so filling one and then sitting down in the dining room is not allowed. Drinks are charged separately and never weighed.

Value comes down to what you scoop. Carved sirloin, shrimp, and chicken give you the most food for the weight. Soup, gravy, and watery vegetables cost the same per pound and give back far less.

Ways to Save

  1. Eat lunch instead of dinner. The same spread runs several dollars cheaper before 4 PM.
  2. Ask for the senior rate. Guests 60 and over save a dollar or two, and the weekday early bird is the best price of the week.
  3. Bring the little ones on a weekday. Kids 3 and under eat free, and the afternoon crowd is calmer.
  4. Join Golden Corral Rewards. You earn one point per dollar and 100 points converts to $10 off. New members get $5 off a $25 purchase through the app, and receipts can be scanned within three days of a visit.
  5. Check the app for limited time offers. Recent ones have included 25 wings for $25 and the sirloin and shrimp combo for $15.49.
  6. Skip the fountain drink if you are watching the bill. At roughly $3.79 each, a family of four adds more than $15 on drinks alone.
  7. Ask about a military discount. Many locations offer one with a valid ID.

Final Thoughts

Golden Corral Colorado Springs works when everyone in the group wants something different and nobody wants to spend $30 a head. Frequently Asked Questions

How much does Golden Corral cost in Colorado Springs? +
Adult lunch usually lands between $11.49 and $13.99, and dinner runs about $16.99 to $19.99. Weekend breakfast sits near $12.99 to $14.49. Seniors pay a dollar or two less, kids 4 to 12 pay less again, and children 3 and under eat free. Drinks are extra at most meals.
What time are senior prices at Golden Corral? +
The senior rate applies all day at most locations, as long as you ask before paying. The best deal is the weekday early bird, usually between 2 and 4 PM, when the senior buffet drops to about $9.99 to $10.99 and often includes a drink. It ends when dinner pricing starts at 4.
How much is Golden Corral per pound? +
Weigh and Pay usually runs $6.99 to $8.99 per pound. A typical box weighs 1.5 to 2.5 pounds, so expect to spend $12 to $22. Each restaurant sets its own rate, and drinks are never included.
Can you go to Golden Corral for lunch and stay for dinner? +
You pay when you walk in, so the price depends on when you pay, not when you leave. Arrive before 4 PM and you are usually charged lunch pricing while the dinner items come out around you. Some locations ask guests to pay the difference if they are still eating at the switch, so ask the cashier before you sit down.
Does this location serve breakfast every day? +
No. Breakfast runs Friday, Saturday, and Sunday from 7:30 AM until about 11. Monday through Thursday the restaurant opens at 10:30 AM for lunch.
Do I need a reservation? +
Not for regular dining. Reservations are only used for group sales of 27 or more and for the private party room, which needs at least a week of notice.
Is there anything for picky kids? +
Plenty. Pizza, mac and cheese, fried chicken, mashed potatoes, and soft serve cover most kids, and the dessert bar settles any remaining debate.
